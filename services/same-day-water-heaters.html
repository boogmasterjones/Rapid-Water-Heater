What to Do When Your Water Heater Fails

If you find water around the base of the tank, the first priority is stopping the flow. There is a shut-off valve on the cold water line entering the top of the unit; closing it stops more water from feeding the tank. If that valve will not turn or there is no valve there, shut off the water to the whole house at the main. On an electric unit, switch off the breaker that serves the water heater. On a gas unit, turn the gas control to the off position. Doing those two things protects both the home and the equipment while you wait.

A tank that is leaking from the body itself cannot be repaired, and no amount of tightening will change that. Once the steel has corroded through, replacement is the only real option, and the leak generally gets worse rather than stabilizing. If the water is instead coming from a fitting at the top or from the relief valve discharge, that may be a smaller repair — which is one of the things we determine on arrival rather than assuming.

When Replacement Beats Repair

Not every failure calls for a new unit. A failed thermostat or heating element on an otherwise sound electric tank is a genuine repair, and on a unit only a few years old that is usually the right answer. The calculation changes with age. Once a tank is past roughly ten years, spending several hundred dollars on a repair buys you an unknown amount of remaining life on a component — the tank itself — that is already near the end of its expected service.

We walk through that honestly rather than defaulting to replacement because it is the larger invoice. If your unit is worth repairing, we will say so. If it is not, we will explain what specifically has failed and why a repair would be throwing good money after bad, so the decision is yours and it is an informed one.

Why Age Matters More Than You Would Think

Most tank water heaters last somewhere between eight and twelve years, and Florida conditions tend to push toward the lower end of that range because of mineral content in the water. The manufacture date is usually encoded in the serial number on the unit's label rather than printed plainly, and the format varies by brand, so it is easy to be years off if you are guessing from memory of when you moved in.

Knowing the real age is genuinely useful. A homeowner who knows their unit is eleven years old can plan a replacement on their own schedule, choose the system they actually want, and avoid the version of this that begins with a cold shower and a wet floor. If you are not sure how to read yours, we are happy to tell you what it says while we are out for anything else.
