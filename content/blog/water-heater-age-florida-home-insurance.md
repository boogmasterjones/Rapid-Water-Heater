## How to Find Your Water Heater's Age

Every water heater has a **data plate**, a label usually on the side of the tank. The plate lists the manufacturer, model number, serial number and specs such as capacity and fuel type.

The manufacture date is usually on the plate, either printed outright or coded into the serial number. Each brand uses its own code. Some use letters for the month and year, and others use the first few digits. To read it:

1. **Find the serial number** on the data plate. Use a flashlight if the heater sits in a tight closet or garage corner.
2. **Take a clear photo** of the whole label.
3. **Look up how that brand reads its serial numbers** on the manufacturer's website or support line. 

Check for paperwork too. 

## Why Insurers Look at Water Heater Age

An older unit can look like a risk to an insurance company, even if it still makes hot water every morning. It's worth knowing your unit's age and condition before your policy comes up for renewal.

### Cutoffs Differ by Carrier

There's no single age limit that applies everywhere. Each insurance company sets its own rules, and they can change. One carrier may be fine with a unit that another flags, and some care more about condition than age. **Before you make any decision, ask your insurance agent** what your carrier accepts and whether an older water heater could affect your coverage or renewal.

## How Long Do Water Heaters Last?

How long a tank water heater lasts depends on water quality, how much hot water your household uses, and how well the unit was installed and maintained.

If your tank is getting older, it's worth planning ahead, even if nothing seems wrong yet. Other signs that it's wearing out include rusty water, rumbling noises, moisture around the base, or running out of hot water sooner than you used to.

## Planned Replacement vs. Emergency Swap

There are two ways to replace a water heater: on your schedule, or on its schedule.

### Planning Ahead

Good times to plan a replacement include:

- **Before an insurance renewal.** Ask your agent what your policy requires, then plan your replacement around it.
- **Before listing your home.** An older water heater can become a talking point with buyers. 
- **When buying a home.** Check the data plate during your inspection period. If the tank is near the end of its life, you'll know before you close, and before you shop for insurance.

Planning ahead gives you time to compare options, get a clear quote and choose the right unit.
