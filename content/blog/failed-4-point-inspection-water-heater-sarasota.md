Your 4-point inspection report came back with a note about the water heater. Maybe it says the unit is too old, or there's rust or a drip. Now you're wondering whether your insurance is at risk and what you need to do.

Take a breath. This is a common situation for Sarasota homeowners, and it can usually be handled with a few clear steps. Here's what the inspection looks at, who to talk to first, and how a replacement works if you need one.

## What a 4-Point Inspection Looks at on a Water Heater

The water heater falls under the plumbing portion of a 4-point inspection. Insurers often request this report for older homes, either when you buy a new policy or at renewal.

For the water heater, the inspector typically records:

- **Age** of the unit, usually based on the manufacturer's data plate
- **General condition**, such as rust, corrosion or visible wear
- **Leaks** at the tank, fittings or connections
- **The temperature and pressure relief valve**, including whether it's present and has a proper discharge line

The inspector doesn't decide whether you keep your coverage. They document what they see, and your insurance carrier decides what to do with that information.

## Age Limits and Deadlines Depend on Your Carrier

This is the part many homeowners find confusing. There is no single rule for how old is "too old." Each insurance carrier sets its own guidelines for water heater age and condition, and they may also set their own deadlines for making a replacement.

So before you do anything else, **call your insurance agent** and ask:

- What exactly did the carrier flag on my water heater?
- Does it need to be replaced, or is there another option?
- Is there a deadline, and what documentation do you need afterward?

Getting those answers in writing helps you plan without guessing. Rapid Water Heater LLC can install a new unit, but we can't speak for your insurer or promise what they'll approve. Your agent is the right source for that.

## Step 1: Check the Data Plate for Your Water Heater's Age

Your water heater has a label, called a data plate, usually on the side of the tank or unit. It lists the model and serial number. On many brands, the manufacture date is coded into the serial number, though each manufacturer uses its own format.

Snap a clear photo of that label. It's useful to have on hand for your insurance agent.

## Step 3: Choose Tank, Tankless or Heat Pump (Hybrid)

A flagged water heater is a chance to think about what fits your home best. We install three main types:

- **Tank water heaters (gas or electric):** The familiar option. A straightforward swap in many homes.
- **Tankless water heaters (gas or electric):** Heat water on demand.
- **Heat pump (hybrid) water heaters:** Electric units that pull heat from the surrounding air to warm the water.

We also install point-of-use and small-capacity units for specific spots in the home.

There's no one right answer. It depends on your household, your current setup and your budget.
