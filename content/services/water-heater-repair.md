        <h2>Water Heater Repair Across Southwest Florida</h2>
        <p>When you search for "water heater repair near me," you want someone who will find the actual problem instead of selling you a new unit by default. Rapid Water Heater LLC is a family-owned, licensed Florida plumbing contractor, and repair calls get the same care as an installation: we diagnose, explain, quote, and then do the work you approve.</p>
        <p>We repair gas and electric tank water heaters and gas and electric tankless units. We offer flexible hours and emergency service, so a heater that quits on a weekend does not have to mean days of cold showers.</p>
        <h3>Electric Water Heater Repair: Elements and Thermostats</h3>
        <p>Most electric tanks in our area have two heating elements, upper and lower, each controlled by its own thermostat, plus a high-limit safety switch that cuts power if the water gets too hot. When an element burns out, the most common sign is hot water that runs out far sooner than it used to. When the upper element or thermostat fails, there is often no hot water at all. We test each element and thermostat with a meter rather than guessing, then replace only the part that has failed.</p>
        <p>A high-limit switch that keeps tripping is a symptom, not the fault. Resetting it may bring the hot water back for a day, but something is overheating the water, usually a stuck thermostat or a shorted element, and that is what we fix.</p>
        <h3>Gas Water Heater Repair: Pilot, Thermocouple and Igniter</h3>
        <p>On a gas unit, no hot water usually comes down to the pilot and the parts around it. A thermocouple or flame sensor that has worn out will let the pilot light but not stay lit. A dirty burner or a blocked air intake can cause the same thing. Newer gas heaters use an electronic igniter and a control board, and those can fail too. We check the gas supply, the venting and the controls, because a gas water heater that is not venting properly is a safety problem, not just an inconvenience.</p>
        <h3>Tankless Water Heater Repair</h3>
        <p>Tankless units fail differently from tanks. Mineral scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, which cuts the flow of hot water and can trigger error codes. Ignition faults, flow sensor problems and venting issues are the other common calls. We work on gas and electric tankless water heaters and tell you plainly whether the unit is worth repairing. If you are weighing a new system instead, our <a href="/services/tankless-water-heater-installation">tankless water heater installation</a> page explains what a replacement involves.</p>
        <h3>Leaks: Which Ones Can Be Repaired</h3>
        <p>Where the water is coming from decides everything. A leak at a supply connection on top of the tank, a drain valve that will not seal, or a temperature and pressure relief valve that drips or discharges are all repairs. A relief valve that keeps opening can also point to pressure in the system that needs an expansion tank, which we check for. A leak from the steel tank itself is different: once the tank has rusted through it cannot be repaired, and the leak only gets worse.</p>
        <h3>When Repair Makes Sense, and When Replacement Does</h3>
        <p>Age matters more than most people expect. Most tank water heaters last somewhere between eight and twelve years, and Florida water tends to push toward the lower end of that range. On a unit only a few years old, a repair is usually the right answer. Past roughly ten years, a repair buys an unknown amount of time on a tank that is already near the end of its expected life. We give you both prices and let you decide.</p>
        <p>If replacement is the better choice, we install traditional tank, tankless and heat pump water heaters from Bradford White, Rheem, A.O. Smith and Halo. See <a href="/services/water-heater-installation">water heater installation and replacement</a>, or <a href="/services/same-day-water-heaters">same-day water heater replacement</a> if you need hot water back fast. Replacement units are typically kept in stock, and every new installation comes with a free 1-year maintenance visit. Our guide to the <a href="/blog/7-signs-its-time-to-replace-your-water-heater">signs it is time to replace your water heater</a> is a useful read before you decide.</p>
        <h3>What to Check Before You Call</h3>
        <p>A few things are safe to check yourself. On an electric unit, look at the breaker for the water heater. On a gas unit, make sure the gas valve to the heater is on and look through the sight glass to see whether the pilot is lit. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility first. If there is water on the floor, close the cold water valve at the top of the tank and switch off the power or gas, then call us at (941) 876-5900.</p>
